He went up to a huge house on the bank of the canal, a house of several stories, and looked at the porter's door. The stairs were dark and narrow. He mounted them slowly, stopping to take breath at every landing. All this time he kept asking himself, "Why am I going? Am I capable of that? Is that serious? Is it real?"


He was a young man of about twenty-three, thin and of medium height, with fine, dark eyes and dark brown hair. His clothes were so shabby that even a man accustomed to shabbiness would have been ashamed to wear them in the street. But the young man was in such a state of nervous irritation that he had long ceased to feel any embarrassment about his clothes, except in certain special cases.

He was morbidly irritable and full of a morbid contempt for himself. He started at every sound, and looked timidly about him, as though he suspected every one of knowing his secret. And yet he was now going to make an experiment, and he was in a state of terrible excitement.

He had, however, been so absorbed in his own thoughts that he might have been walking along without any consciousness of his surroundings. Only with difficulty could he bring himself to attend to anything, and the longer his brooding lasted, the less he was aware of everything before him in the street.
