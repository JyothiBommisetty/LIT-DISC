The nights are clear, but suffused with the monotony of the cicadas and the smell of the river. The river, the Meenachal, which is hardly a river at all during this time of year, is a thin, weedy ribbon threading through the sand. On its banks, the chappathis and the kathappurathu and the kanjira trees stand in patient rows, waiting for the monsoon. The air is thick with the smell of rotting vegetation and the sound of the cicadas, which never stop. It is a sound that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere, a sound that fills the silence and becomes the silence itself.


May in Ayemenem is a hot, brooding month. The days are long and humid. The river shrinks and black crows gorge on bright mangoes in still, dustgreen trees. Red bananas ripen. Jackfruits burst. Dissolute bluebottles hum vacuously in the fruity air. Then they stun themselves against clear windowpanes and die, fatty baffled in the sun.
