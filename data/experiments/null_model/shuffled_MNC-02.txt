I must say I was astonished to learn that the place to which we had been sent was what I have heard it called, a "graveyard." I had no idea. We were simply told to go to a certain address, and there we found ourselves among these large stone boxes with words carved on them. It seemed a strange place to put us, and I said so to my wife, who was standing beside me, looking about with an expression of bewilderment. We had been told, you see, that we would be going to a kind of way station, where we would wait until it was time to go on. But this did not seem like any way station I had ever seen or heard described. There were no benches, no waiting room, no one to direct us. Just these stone boxes in rows, and a cold wind blowing. My wife shivered, and I put my arm around her. It was then that I noticed that I could see through my own arm, and through her as well.
