Emma was standing with her back against the panelling, her shoulders bare, her hair falling loose. Her eyes were fixed on the ceiling, and she was speaking in a low, rapid voice. "I have loved you. I have loved you more than anything in the world. But I cannot go on. I cannot. You must understand." She stopped, and for a moment there was only the sound of the rain against the window. Then she turned to him, and he saw that her face was wet with tears. "I have dreamed of a life beyond this one," she said, "a life of passion and beauty and freedom. But I have only found disappointment." She walked to the window and looked out at the grey sky. The garden was sodden, the leaves fallen. Everything seemed to be dying. She felt a great emptiness inside her, a void that nothing could fill. She had tried romance, and it had failed her. She had tried luxury, and it had bored her. She had tried religion, and it had offered no comfort. There was nothing left.
