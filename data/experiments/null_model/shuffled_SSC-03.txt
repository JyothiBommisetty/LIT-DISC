I hung up the phone. Immediately afterwards, I recognised the voice that had answered in German. It was that of Captain Richard Madden. Madden's presence in Viktor Runeberg's flat meant the end of our anxieties and—but this seemed, or should have seemed, very secondary to me—also the end of our lives. It meant that Runeberg had been arrested or murdered. Before the sun set on this same day, I would share the same fate. Madden was implacable. Rather, to be accurate, he was obliged to be implacable. An Irishman at the service of the English, a man accused of lukewarmness and of being a traitor, how could he not welcome and seize upon this miraculous proof: the capture of two spies of the German Reich? I went up to my room; absurdly I locked the door. I threw myself on my back on the narrow iron cot. Through the window I saw the familiar roofs. I thought of myself as a man lost in a labyrinth of time, where past and future were intertwined in ways I could not comprehend. The garden of forking paths was before me, and I had to choose.
