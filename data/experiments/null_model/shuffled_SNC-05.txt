"At the Hotel Montoya."

"Yes. We came for the fiesta."

"Where are you staying?"

"Two glasses of wine."

"Wine is good here?"

She filled two glasses from a tap on a hogshead and set them on the bar.

"Oh, yes. That's very nice. The bulls are very good this year."

She shrugged. "It's all right. You can get good wine in Pamplona."

"Yes. It's a very nice town."

"Oh, yes. That's a good hotel. You will see the bulls, I suppose?"

She wiped the bar with a cloth. She was not interested in talking. The man beside me finished his wine and went out.


"Do you want anything?" she asked in Spanish.

"It's a nice town," I said.

The wine shop was a typical Navarre wine shop. The shelves were full of wine-skins. There were the usual young men in their shirtsleeves sitting at the tables outside. And inside, in the cool, smelling of the sawdust and the leather of the wine-skins, there was a woman at the bar. She was old and sunken-faced, and she wore a black dress. A man stood at the bar, drinking wine.
