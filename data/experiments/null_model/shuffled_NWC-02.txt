Ah Q was a man of unknown origins. No one knew his family name, his given name, or even his place of birth. He lived in the Wei Zhuang ancestral temple, where he did odd jobs for the villagers. He was not a bad worker, but he had a quick temper and a quick tongue. When someone offended him, he would glare and say, "You are my son, and I am your father." This was his way of winning every argument, for who could deny such a simple logic?

He had been in Wei Zhuang for many years, and everyone knew him. But no one respected him. When he walked through the village, the children would taunt him, and the women would laugh behind his back. He did not seem to mind. He had a theory of self-consolation: if he could not win a real victory, he would win a spiritual one. He would imagine himself victorious, and the world would be set right again. This was his great discovery, the secret of his happiness.
