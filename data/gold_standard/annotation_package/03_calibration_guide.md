# LIT-DISC Calibration Guide

## Training Session for Expert Annotators

---

### Session Overview

The calibration session brings all annotators together (in person or via video call) to establish a shared understanding of the scoring rubric before independent annotation begins. The session takes approximately 60 minutes.

**Agenda:**

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 5 min | Review of the study purpose and workflow |
| 10 min | Walk-through of the Annotation Manual |
| 10 min | Practice Passage 1: Individual scoring, then group discussion |
| 10 min | Practice Passage 2: Individual scoring, then group discussion |
| 10 min | Practice Passage 3: Individual scoring, then group discussion |
| 10 min | Disagreement resolution calibration |
| 5 min | Questions and sign-off |

---

### Practice Passage 1: Low Richness

**Instructions:** Read the passage below. Score each of the seven axes independently. Then compare with the group.

> The meeting was called to order at 9:00 AM. The chairman introduced the first item on the agenda, which was the quarterly budget review. The treasurer presented the figures, which showed a modest increase in revenue and a slight decrease in expenses. There was some discussion about the marketing budget. Mr. Smith proposed that it be increased. Ms. Jones seconded the motion. The motion was put to a vote and carried. The meeting adjourned at 10:30 AM.

**Estimated scores:**

| Axis | Score | Rationale |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Semantic (S) | 1 | Generic vocabulary; no figurative language; purely denotative; no register variation |
| Narrative (N) | 1 | Chronological; uniform pacing; no tension; no structural devices |
| Interaction (I) | 2 | Dialogue exists but is purely informational; no subtext; all speakers interchangeable; procedural formality |
| Symbolic (Sy) | 1 | No recurring motifs or symbolic objects; purely functional description |
| Evolution (E) | 1 | No character arc; no change or development |
| Emotion (Em) | 1 | No affective content; neutral tone throughout |
| Overall (O) | 1 | Functional prose with no literary ambition; no dimension rises above minimal presence |

**Discussion points:**
- Note that Interaction (I) is not 1: there is dialogue, but it is minimal. Score 2 reflects presence without richness.
- Is the passage completely devoid of richness, or does the formal register of meeting minutes carry any literary interest? (Expected consensus: minimal.)
- Confidence should be high (4-5) because the passage clearly lacks richness in all dimensions.

### Practice Passage 2: Moderate-Mixed Richness

**Instructions:** Read the passage below. Notice how some dimensions are high while others are low.

> She stood at the window, watching the rain fall on the garden. The roses were drooping, their petals heavy with water. She thought of the day they had planted them, three years ago, when the garden was new and everything seemed possible. Now the weeds had taken over the vegetable patch, and the fence needed painting. She had meant to fix it, but somehow the weeks had passed and she had not. The rain continued to fall. Somewhere a door banged. She did not turn around.

**Estimated scores:**

| Axis | Score | Rationale |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Semantic (S) | 3 | Competent but not exceptional vocabulary; some sensory description (roses, rain, weeds); no striking figurative language |
| Narrative (N) | 3 | Temporal shift (memory to present); mild tension (the banging door); understated structure |
| Interaction (I) | 1 | No dialogue; solitary passage |
| Symbolic (Sy) | 4 | Roses as symbol of lost hope; rain as melancholy; weeds as neglect — all functioning together symbolically |
| Evolution (E) | 3 | Implied change (from hope to resignation); the gap between "three years ago" and now represents a trajectory |
| Emotion (Em) | 4 | Melancholy, regret, resignation; communicated through indirection (not stating feelings directly) |
| Overall (O) | 3 | Mixed profile: high Symbolic and moderate Emotion but low Interaction and moderate others |

**Discussion points:**
- The passage shows a typical pattern: Symbolic and Emotion are higher than Interaction (solitary scene) and Narrative (minimal plot). This mixed profile is common and expected.
- Does the low Interaction score lower the overall? (Expected: yes, but the overall is higher than the average because the Symbolic-Emotion coupling is effective.)
- Note that confidence might be lower (3) because the understated quality makes scores harder to pin down.

### Practice Passage 3: High Richness

**Instructions:** Read the passage below. High richness across most dimensions.

> He looked at his hands. They were old now, spotted and thin-skinned, the veins raised like blue rivers on a faded map. These hands had held his children when they were born, had gripped the rail of a ship crossing the Atlantic, had signed papers that changed lives, had touched her face on the morning she left. He spread his fingers wide. The joints ached. He remembered the day he had learned to play the piano, how the keys had felt cold and unfamiliar, and how, years later, his hands had moved across them without thought, as if the music lived in his bones. He closed his hands into fists, then opened them again. Empty now. But they had been full.

**Estimated scores:**

| Axis | Score | Rationale |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Semantic (S) | 6 | Striking figurative language ("blue rivers on a faded map"); precise, layered vocabulary; extended metaphor of hands-as-history; rhythmic prose |
| Narrative (N) | 5 | Temporal compression (entire life in one paragraph); flashback structure; tension between past fullness and present emptiness |
| Interaction (I) | 1 | Solitary; no dialogue |
| Symbolic (Sy) | 6 | Hands as symbol of a life lived, of agency, of loss; piano as symbol of mastery and effort rewarded; empty hands as symbol of mortality |
| Evolution (E) | 6 | Arc from birth of children through migration, career, love, loss, to old age — a complete life trajectory compressed into a paragraph |
| Emotion (Em) | 6 | Complex mix: nostalgia, pride, regret, acceptance, grief; communicated through bodily sensation and memory rather than statement |
| Overall (O) | 6 | Despite zero Interaction, the passage achieves extraordinary richness through the fusion of Symbolic, Evolution, and Emotion dimensions |

**Discussion points:**
- This is a case where overall is high despite one zero dimension (Interaction). The computational LDRI would reflect this through the coupling mechanism.
- Is the Semantic score a 6 or a 7? (The group should debate: is there an extra layer — an allusion, a wordplay — that would push it to 7? Consensus may vary.)
- Note that Evolution is high even though no "event" happens in the passage — the evolution is narrative compression.
- Confidence should be high (4-5).

### Disagreement Resolution Procedure

When annotators disagree on a score (difference of 2 or more points), follow this procedure:

**Step 1: Clarify (2 min).** Each annotator explains their rationale in one sentence, referencing the Annotation Manual.

**Step 2: Identify source (2 min).** Determine whether the disagreement is:
- **Definitional**: annotators disagree on what the axis means → consult the Manual
- **Perceptual**: annotators see different features in the text → re-read the passage aloud
- **Valuation**: annotators agree on features but weigh them differently → acknowledge as legitimate difference

**Step 3: Calibrate (2 min).** The facilitator suggests a compromise or clarifies which interpretation aligns with the study's operational definitions. The goal is not forced consensus but shared understanding.

**Step 4: Record (1 min).** Note the disagreement in the session log. During independent annotation, annotators may score according to their own judgment.

**Guiding principle.** Calibration aims to reduce arbitrary variation without eliminating legitimate critical disagreement. A difference of 1 point is acceptable. Differences of 2+ points should be discussed.

---

### During Independent Annotation

After calibration, annotators proceed independently. If uncertainty arises during annotation:

1. Consult this Calibration Guide to review the discussion of similar passages
2. Consult the Annotation Manual for detailed axis definitions
3. Use the confidence scale honestly (low confidence is informative data)
4. Add notes explaining the source of uncertainty

**Remember:** Your expert judgment is the primary instrument of this study. The calibration is a tuning exercise, not a prescription of "correct" answers.
