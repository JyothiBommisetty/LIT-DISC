I grew up in a poor neighborhood in Naples, in a city that was still recovering from the war. My father was a porter at the city hall, my mother was a housewife, and they had four children. I was the eldest. My mother had wanted a boy, and when I turned out to be a girl, she was disappointed. She never said so, but I knew. She made me understand in a thousand small ways: the way she looked at me, the way she spoke to me, the way she touched me. She was not cruel, but she was distant. I learned early that I would have to make my own way in the world, that no one would help me, that I would have to be strong and intelligent and determined. And so I was. I studied hard, I read everything I could find, I taught myself to write. And when I met Lila, I knew that she was the sister I had never had, the friend who would understand everything without my having to explain.
