It was said that a new person had appeared on the sea-front, a lady with a little dog. Dmitri Dmitritch Gurov, who had been a fortnight in Yalta and was used to the place, had begun to take an interest in new arrivals. Sitting in the pavilion at Vernet's, he saw a young lady in a white dress walking along the sea-front. She passed close by him, and he noticed her fair hair and her dark eyes. He followed her, not knowing why, drawn by some vague attraction. She stopped at a bench near the water and sat down, looking out at the sea. The sun was setting, and the sky was a pale gold. Gurov lit a cigarette and watched her.

The lady turned and looked at him. He saw that she was young, perhaps twenty, with a sensitive face and a shy expression. There was something melancholy in her gaze, as if she were thinking of something distant and sad. She looked away, and he felt a sudden desire to know her, to speak to her, to understand what she was thinking. He approached her and said, "Excuse me, but I believe I have seen you before."
