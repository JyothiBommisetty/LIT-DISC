Fiona lived in her parents' house, in the town where she and I had grown up. She took care of them until they died, and then she lived on in the house. I had moved away, but I came back often. We had known each other since we were children. We had played together, gone to school together, fallen in love together. But we had never married. There had been a war, and then there had been other people, and then it was too late. Or perhaps it wasn't too late. When we were in our sixties, we began to see each other differently. We began to meet for coffee, then for dinner, then for weekends. We talked about everything and nothing. We were comfortable together. It seemed natural to move in together, and we did. I sold my house and moved into hers. We were happy. Then she began to forget things. At first it was small things, where she had put her keys, what day it was. Then it was bigger things. She would look at me sometimes as if she had never seen me before.
