The pale young gentleman touched his heart with his hand, and looked at me, as if he would have said, "You don't know me then?" He had a beautiful face, and a pretty and to a certain point, exactly proportioned figure. That figure was made of two parts: the upper part of the body was that of a young man, and the lower part was that of a boy. But he could not be a boy, for his figure was that of a young man, and it was on the whole a very pretty young man's figure.

"Mr. Pip?" said he.

"I am Mr. Pip."

"Mr. Joe Gargery's adopted son, I suppose?"

"Yes."

"My name is Herbert, Mr. Pip, and I am a fellow of the same college—or rather the same sort of college. I am a student at the Hammersmith Seminary, and it is my duty to inform you that I am your tutor."

I looked at him with great surprise.

"To think," said he, "that I should have been the innocent cause of his being so badly bruised, and that I should have been the first to learn of his being such a criminal!" I could not have said a word, for my voice was quite gone.

"My dear Mr. Pip," said he, "this is very pleasant. You have a good home here, and I am sure, a very comfortable one. And now, I will tell you what I have to say. I am commissioned to make you an offer of a very liberal nature. Your guardian, in consideration of your being brought up by Mr. and Mrs. Joe Gargery, has decided to settle upon you a sum of money which will enable you to become a gentleman. I think you had better come into the house, and we will talk the matter over."
