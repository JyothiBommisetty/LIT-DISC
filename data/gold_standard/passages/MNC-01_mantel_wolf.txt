Felled, dazed, silent, he has fallen; knocked full length on the cobbles of the yard. His head turns sideways; his eyes are turned towards the gate, as if someone might arrive to help him. He is lying in a pool of his own blood, which is seeping crookedly into the cracks in the stones. Blood is bubbling out of his nose. He is a skinny boy, perhaps eleven or twelve years old. His clothes are poor, but his hands are those of a gentleman, with long fingers, and his skin is very white. A man in a leather apron stands over him, holding a wooden staff. He has beaten him, and now he stands back, as if to assess his work. Around them, a circle of men and boys are watching. Someone says, "Enough." Someone says, "He has had enough." The boy's father, a prosperous merchant, stands at the edge of the crowd. He does not step forward. He looks as if he wishes he were somewhere else. The boy's name is Thomas Cromwell.
