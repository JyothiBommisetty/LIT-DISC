Many things combine to create a resemblance between Midaq Alley and a camel. The narrow, arched entrance resembles the camel's neck, and the alley itself, with its uneven walls leaning inward, resembles the camel's body. But perhaps the most striking resemblance is the way the alley, like a camel, seems to carry the weight of the world on its back.

At the heart of the alley is the café of Kirsha, a man with a passion for hashish and young men. Around him the regulars gather: Abbas the barber, a gentle soul dreaming of love; Hamida, the beautiful and ambitious young woman who knows she was meant for more than this alley; Dr. Booshy, the dentist whose methods are unorthodox but effective; and the others who make this alley their world. They sit in the café, drinking tea and coffee, smoking, talking, and watching the world pass by. The alley is their universe, and they cannot imagine any other. And yet, beyond the arched entrance, the modern world presses in, bringing with it change and uncertainty.
